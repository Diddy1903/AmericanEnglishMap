/ɪ/ as in "kit"
/ei/ as in "face"*, /ii/ as in "fleece"
*In pane-pain unmerged accents /ei/ is two phonemes,
/ei/ and /e:/, derived from Middle English /ai/ and /a:/

Central Vowels

/ʌ/ as in "strut",/ʊ/ as in "put",/ə/ as in "commA"*
*Many Caribbean creoles lack central /ə/, using unreduced vowels instead. Merger of
unstressed /ow/ (e.g. "yellow") and /ə/ is more widespread in the South than the North.

Western Pennsylvania Vowel Shift:
Monophthongal /au/, lowered /ʌ/

Low Back Vowels

tense: /a:/ as in "father", /o/ as in "lot", /ɔ:/ as in "thought"
*The /a:/ phoneme, distinct from /æ/ is absent prior to the late 18th century.

Low back merger (cot-caught merger):
merger of /o/ and /ɔ:/

 dashed line: /æ/ raising
 thin solid line: /o/ fronting
thick solid line:  /ɛ/ and /ʌ/ retraction
(/ɔ:/ lowering was initially described as a stage of the shift,
however this is disputed by later studies)

Non-front Diphthongs

/uu/ as in "goose", /ou/ as in "goat"*, /au/ as in "mouth"
/ai/ as in "price", /oi/ as in "choice"
*For accents with New England Short-O, /ou/ is two phonemes, /ow/ and /ɵ/, derived from Middle English /ow/ and /o:/

Vowels before R

/ɜr/ as in "nurse",/ər/ as in lettER*
/i:r/ as in "near",/e:r/ as in "stair", /a:r/ as in "star"
/u:r/ as in "cure", /o:r/ as in "force", /ɔ:r/ as in "north"
*/ɜr/ and /ər/ are absent in 1700s, with /ʌ+r/ being used instead (Grandgent, 1892). This is also the case in Jamaican and Guyanese Creole.

Conditional Mergers

Vowel mergers before /n/,/l/,/g/ and after alveolars

/el/-/eil/, /il/-/iil/: FELL-FAIL & FILL-FEEL mergers
/ul/-/uwl/,/uwl/-/owl/, GULL-GOAL,POOL-PULL mergers
/aun/=/^ng/ ng-assimilation, "down"-"dung" homphones
/æg/-/εg/-/eig/: BAG-VAGUE merger
/ɔʃ/=/ɔɪʃ/,/ɔ:rʃ/ upglided
/in/=/εn/: PIN-PEN merger

Yod-dropping: /iu/-/uu/ merger after /t/,/d/,/n/
(yod insertion in 'car' /kjar/ 'garden' /gjarden/, included for contrast)

L-deletion in clusters: /l/ is absent before labials, /p//b//f//v/
