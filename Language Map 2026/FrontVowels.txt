The short front vowels /æ/, /ɛ/ and /ɪ/, and the long front vowels /ei/ and /ii/, show a variety of chain shifts triggered by movement of the /a:/ vowel or by the monophthonging of /ai/.

While, not a simple upwards or downward shift, the Northern Cities Vowel Shift in the Great Lakes region does affect the short front vowels. The raised /æ/ pronunciation in this region, triggers a counter-clockwise shift in the mid and low vowels (see the chart) in the following steps:
Northern Cities Vowel Shift occurs under the following steps.
	•	Stage 1: /æ/ is fronted to [ɛə~iə] in all contexts
	•	Stage 2: /o:/ is fronted to [ä:]
	•	Stage 3 (disputed): /ɔ:/ is lowered to [ɑ]
	•	Stage 4: /ə/ is backed to [ɔ]
	•	Stage 5: /ɛ/ is lowered and backed to [ə]
	•	Stage 6: /ɪ/ is lowered and backed to [ɛ]
Dialects without a BATH split or any chain shifts, can show a few other shifts. /a/ can show minimal variation, particularly in Latino Englishes. The Western system, where /æ/ is tenser before nasals than elsewhere, and raising before /g/. /e/ and /i/ can also merge before /n/ in the south.
